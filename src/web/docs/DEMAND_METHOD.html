Population and household scenarios

Household change, existing problems, policy-enabled production and unverified new-building need answer different questions.

Population continuation

P2030 = max(0, P2025 + 5 × mean(births − deaths
  + domestic migration + international migration))

Alternative windows use 2023–2025, 2021–2025 and 2025. The central scenario uses three years. The envelope is their minimum/maximum, not a confidence interval or all possible futures. Residual adjustments reconcile historical accounting but are not projected as a demographic mechanism.

Household change

change = (P2030 − P2025) × (1 − group-quarter share)
  ÷ average household size

Size and group-quarter share stay fixed. The synthetic 2025 household base is not a published 2025 estimate. Cohorts are not aged, headship rates are not estimated and migration-age composition is not forecast. Geographies use their own inputs, so household proxies and envelope endpoints do not add exactly across locations.

Why this is not construction need

One net new household implies one extra occupied unit in the accounting identity, conditional on assumptions. Existing usable supply, vacancies, conversions and completions can meet part of that change. Replacement/accessibility needs can persist amid decline. Crowding and HUD problems overlap other measures and are not independent missing-home counts.

A construction requirement needs verified suppressed formation, losses, turnover reserves, usable excess vacancies, expected completions and net conversions on the same geographic/time basis. Missing required inputs keep it unavailable; no default shortage allocation or permit-to-completion multiplier is inserted.

Uncertainty

ACS 2020–2024 is a multiyear survey. Original 90% margins remain; combined margins use approximate root-sum-square without covariance. Scenario bounds do not propagate all survey/formation uncertainty. Short revised-vintage tests and California/NC projection comparisons do not validate five-year household forecasts. See validation.
