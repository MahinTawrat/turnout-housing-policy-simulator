Model and assumptions

Choose a place, policy and assumption setting. The tool estimates possible additional homes over 2026–2030, explains the inputs and identifies local evidence to check. It covers the United States, 50 states and DC, and 3,144 counties or county equivalents.

How to read the result. This is a conditional scenario, not promised construction, a shortage count or proof of the best local reform. A permit is permission to build. The five reforms can affect the same projects and must not be added together.

Where the baseline comes from

Census reports 1,431,616 privately owned housing units authorized in 2025. The selected continuation holds that annual pace flat: 1,431,616 × 5 = 7,158,080 reference units. Minnesota uses 20,947 × 5 = 104,735. This five-year permit reference is separate from existing housing stock.

Four candidates—latest year, three-year average, five-year average and damped five-year trend—were compared on declared historical development folds. Development tests selected the latest-year method before the separate 2021–2025 holdout. A flat future line is the selected continuation, not a chart error. The unchanged-policy baseline is an assumption, not an inventory of current local laws.

State forecasts are evaluated historically. County references allocate state totals using 2025 permit shares; they are not independently tested local forecasts. Refreshed historical-error stress ranges are not confidence intervals or guaranteed bounds.

The five calculations

The chain is official observation → why used → response assumption → calculation → possible extra homes. See all equations and Low/Default/High values.

PolicyMeasured input and reasonDefault calculation
ADUACS detached homes proxy the stock where an extra home could be considered; they are not eligible parcels.Detached homes × 1% five-year participation, rounded by county.
Missing middleDetached stock proxies opportunities to replace one home with a small building.Detached homes × 0.25% redevelopment × 2 net extra homes, rounded by county.
FeesPermits and valuation proxy project activity and value.Reference × min(30%, $25,000 saving ÷ value per unit) × 0.5 response.
TransitTract housing exposure represents the share near qualifying stops.Reference × exposure share × 25% uplift.
ParkingPermits and valuation proxy affected projects and value.Reference × 50% affected × min(30%, 0.4 spaces × $30,000 ÷ value per unit) × 0.5 response.

Participation, redevelopment, uplift, affected share and response are sensitivity assumptions, not fitted causal coefficients or probabilities. Fee savings are the standardized $50,000 benchmark cut in half. Local fees, remaining voluntary parking, binding restrictions and full costs are unverified. Parking assumptions labeled 2026 dollars and 2025 permit valuations lack a common-dollar correction.

County results use ties-to-even rounding. State ADU/missing-middle results sum rounded counties. State fee/parking totals also reconcile county coverage to official state permits. State transit uses direct state exposure. National totals sum states/DC. Missing required observations remain unavailable.

How transit exposure is reconstructed

The official reconstruction selects documented BTS fixed-guideway mode tokens, unions 804.672-metre geodesic stop buffers and intersects them with TIGER tracts in an explicit equal-area projection. Housing is weighted by the intersected tract-area share. This assumes uniform housing distribution across the full tract, including water geometry. It is not a walking catchment, parcel eligibility check or observed production near stops.

Version 3 uses PyShp, GeographicLib and PyClipper. WGS84 geodesic buffers are projected to a spherical equal-area surface; NAD83 tract coordinates are treated as WGS84 without datum-offset correction. Integer clipping uses 1 mm precision. Source hashes, projection choices and differences are in the manifest and exposure comparison. Display maps do not alter analytical geometry.

Missing official observations. Census Errata 148 identifies 14 withheld New York tracts. Suffolk County, New York and U.S. transit point results stay unavailable. They are not filled with zero, another vintage or an undisclosed partial sum. The other four reforms remain available.

Household change and existing housing problems

Population scenarios continue births minus deaths plus domestic and international migration using 2023–2025, 2021–2025 and 2025 windows. The envelope is the minimum/maximum of those variants, not probability bounds. Household change equals population change × (1 − group-quarter share) ÷ average household size, holding size and group-quarter share constant.

These continuations are separate from production equations. ACS crowding, rent, bedrooms and HUD problems describe existing conditions. They are not added to growth or used as automatic production multipliers. New-building need remains unavailable without verified vacancy reserves, usable vacancies, expected completions, losses, conversions and independent suppressed formation. See demand methods and limits.

Reproduce the release

python scripts/build.py
node tests/test_tool.cjs
python scripts/verify.py
python scripts/package.py

The default build recomputes results from pinned official processed inputs. Expected answers are separate regression fixtures, never model inputs. To download and clean official raw data, install requirements-spatial.txt, then run python scripts/refresh.py. Large raw files stay in an excluded cache. Revised sources require explicit revision handling.

python scripts/build.py --mode frozen reproduces the earlier approved figures. The current transit reconstruction is a documented replacement for an incomplete inherited extraction trail, not exact recovery.
