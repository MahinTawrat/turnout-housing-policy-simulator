Minnesota policy comparison

Minnesota's Default five-year permit reference is 104,735: 20,947 units authorized in 2025 × five years. This ranks modeled possible additions under chosen assumptions, not the best policy for every Minnesota community.

The framing matters

These are five separate “what if” comparisons. The largest increment reflects the stock, exposure and assumptions in its equation; it does not prove the greatest affordability benefit or strongest need match. Modest statewide transit additions can coexist with useful station-area priorities. Fee and parking reforms can affect several building types but do not set rents.

Participation and redevelopment move stock-based estimates directly. Valuation proxies, cost response, avoided parking and affected share govern cost reforms. Transit depends on exposure and uplift. Low/High settings are sensitivity cases, not probabilities or guaranteed bounds. Zero actual additional production remains possible.

Check current local rules, sites, owner/developer interest, finance, infrastructure, intended bedrooms and rents. Existing housing pressure is separate evidence and does not establish delivery of matching homes. Do not add the five outputs or claim a shortage closed.
